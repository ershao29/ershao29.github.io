This summary condenses the original Chinese notes. The paper is a theoretical study of signed distances, boundary projections, convexity, subdifferentials, and monotone operators. It does not introduce or benchmark a neutron-transport algorithm. PDF page pp corresponds to journal page 750+p750+p.

1. Definitions and sign convention

For a nonempty proper subset S⊂ℝnS\subset\mathbb R^n, let dSd_S denote ordinary Euclidean distance and use negative inside:

DS(x)=dS(x)−dSc(x),|DS(x)|=d∂S(x).D_S(x)=d_S(x)-d_{S^c}(x),\qquad |D_S(x)|=d_{\partial S}(x).

The negative value measures interior boundary depth, unlike ordinary distance, which vanishes throughout the set. For a radius-2 disk, DS(x)=∥x∥−2D_S(x)=\|x\|-2.

intS={DS<0},clS={DS≤0},∂S={DS=0}.\operatorname{int}S=\{D_S<0\},\quad \operatorname{cl}S=\{D_S\le0\},\quad \partial S=\{D_S=0\}.

For nonclosed sets, DS≤0D_S\le0 recovers the closure, not necessarily the original membership of boundary points. Proposition 3.5 states that equal SDFs are equivalent to equal closures and equal interiors. Thus open and closed balls have the same SDF. This topological equivalence differs from equality almost everywhere.

Useful identities include DSc=−DSD_{S^c}=-D_S, DS+w(x)=DS(x−w)D_{S+w}(x)=D_S(x-w), and DkS(x)=kDS(x/k)D_{kS}(x)=kD_S(x/k) for k>0k>0. General nonuniform scaling does not obey the same distance rule.

2. Lipschitz and Eikonal properties

Every such SDF is 1-Lipschitz:

|DS(x)−DS(y)|≤∥x−y∥.|D_S(x)-D_S(y)|\le\|x-y\|.

Theorem 3.1 characterizes unit gradient almost everywhere by zero nn-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the boundary. Convex proper sets satisfy the required boundary condition. Lipschitz continuity alone does not imply unit gradient almost everywhere: for S=ℚnS=\mathbb Q^n, both set and complement are dense, DS=0D_S=0, and the boundary is all space.

At a differentiable nonboundary point with nearest boundary point qq,

∇DS(x)=x−qDS(x),q=x−DS(x)∇DS(x).\nabla D_S(x)=\frac{x-q}{D_S(x)},\qquad q=x-D_S(x)\nabla D_S(x).

The signed denominator gives the outward gradient inside as well as outside. At a medial-axis point, the gradient may not exist; a vanishing numerical gradient is not a substitute for set-valued geometry.

3. Projection, normals, and skeleton

Write PSP_S for projection onto the set and QS=P∂SQ_S=P_{\partial S} for projection onto the boundary. For a closed convex proper set CC, exterior projection is unique, while interior boundary projection may be multivalued. In a unit disk, PC(0)=0P_C(0)=0, whereas QC(0)Q_C(0) is the entire unit circle.

The normal decomposition is

x=q+DC(x)n,q∈QC(x),n∈𝕊∩NC(q),x=q+D_C(x)n,\qquad q\in Q_C(x),\quad n\in\mathbb S\cap N_C(q),

where NCN_C is the outward normal cone. The skeleton comprises interior points with multiple closest boundary points. It is empty exactly for half-spaces or convex sets with empty ambient interior. The paper's auxiliary set V(C)V(C) contains the skeleton; general equality was not established.

Projection onto any nonempty proper closed set is paramonotone (Theorem 3.5), even without convexity. For the closed convex CC considered here, boundary projection is maximally monotone exactly when its skeleton is empty (Theorem 3.6). Paramonotonicity does not imply a single-valued continuous projection.

4. Convexity and subdifferentials

For closed convex proper CC, support function σC\sigma_C, and unit sphere 𝕊\mathbb S,

DC(x)=sup∥v∥=1{⟨v,x⟩−σC(v)}=(σC+ι𝕊)*(x).D_C(x)=\sup_{\|v\|=1}\{\langle v,x\rangle-\sigma_C(v)\} =(\sigma_C+\iota_{\mathbb S})^*(x).

A supremum of affine functions is convex. Directions with infinite support value make no contribution. The formula requires all unit directions, not merely a finite list of polyhedral face normals.

For a polyhedron C={x:aiTx≤bi}C=\{x:a_i^Tx\le b_i\} with normalized aia_i,

DC(x)=maxi(aiTx−bi)(x∈C).D_C(x)=\max_i(a_i^Tx-b_i)\quad(x\in C).

Outside, compute the actual distance to the feasible polyhedron, for example through Euclidean projection. At (2,2)(2,2) outside [−1,1]2[-1,1]^2, maximum constraint violation is 1 while distance is 2\sqrt2. This is a useful counterexample to treating Boolean max as an exact SDF everywhere.

Theorem 3.8 gives

∂DC(x)={{(x−PC(x))/DC(x)},x∉C,conv(𝕊∩NC(x)),x∈∂C,conv{(x−q)/DC(x):q∈QC(x)},x∈intC.\partial D_C(x)= \begin{cases} \{(x-P_C(x))/D_C(x)\},&x\notin C,\\ \operatorname{conv}(\mathbb S\cap N_C(x)),&x\in\partial C,\\ \operatorname{conv}\{(x-q)/D_C(x):q\in Q_C(x)\},&x\in\operatorname{int}C. \end{cases}

At a unit-ball center, the subdifferential is the whole unit ball; the function is not differentiable there. At a square center, the subdifferential is a diamond. Ordinary distance instead has ∂dC(x)=𝔹∩NC(x)\partial d_C(x)=\mathbb B\cap N_C(x) for x∈Cx\in C; these boundary formulas are different.

For nonempty interior, SDF minimizers satisfy

x∈intC∩convQC(x).x\in\operatorname{int}C\cap\operatorname{conv}Q_C(x).

When attained, they are largest-inscribed-ball centers; they need not be unique. An unbounded half-space has no minimizer.

5. Range results and a convex-analysis construction

Let A=𝕊∩domσCA=\mathbb S\cap\operatorname{dom}\sigma_C and B=𝕊∩ranNCB=\mathbb S\cap\operatorname{ran}N_C. Theorems 3.10–3.11 enclose the SDF subdifferential range between appropriate relative interiors and convex hulls of these sets, within the unit ball. Finite support values and attained support normals differ for unbounded sets. For compact convex sets the range is the whole unit ball. The range is already realized on the boundary and skeleton (Theorem 3.13).

A key construction uses

F(t,x)={max{DC(x),−t},t≥0,+∞,t<0.F(t,x)=\begin{cases}\max\{D_C(x),-\sqrt t\},&t\ge0,\\+\infty,&t<0.\end{cases}

For closed convex CC with interior,

dom∂F=(ℝ+×ℝn)\({0}×intC).\operatorname{dom}\partial F= (\mathbb R_+\times\mathbb R^n)\setminus (\{0\}\times\operatorname{int}C).

Although FF is convex, its subdifferential domain can be nonconvex: choosing C=[−1,1]C=[-1,1] retains (0,−1),(0,1)(0,-1),(0,1) but removes their midpoint. The effective function domain remains convex. This is an application in convex/monotone-operator theory, not a geometric tracking algorithm.

6. Explicit cone examples

For a proper closed convex cone KK, its SDF is the support function of conv(K⊖∩𝕊)\operatorname{conv}(K^{\ominus}\cap\mathbb S), hence positively homogeneous and subadditive. It may take negative values despite being sublinear.

For the nonnegative orthant,

Dℝ+n(x)={∥(min(x1,0),…,min(xn,0))∥,x∉ℝ+n,−minixi,x∈ℝ+n.D_{\mathbb R_+^n}(x)= \begin{cases} \|(\min(x_1,0),\ldots,\min(x_n,0))\|,&x\notin\mathbb R_+^n,\\ -\min_i x_i,&x\in\mathbb R_+^n. \end{cases}

For the second-order cone K={(z,t):t≥∥z∥}K=\{(z,t):t\ge\|z\|\}, with r=∥z∥r=\|z\|,

DK(z,t)={r2+t2,t≤−r,(r−t)/2,t>−r.D_K(z,t)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{r^2+t^2},&t\le-r,\\(r-t)/\sqrt2,&t>-r.\end{cases}

The accompanying Chinese notes complete omitted projection/subgradient branches in the printed example. For a proper linear subspace, ambient interior is empty and SDF equals ordinary nonnegative distance, such as |y||y| for the x-axis.

8. Corrections and thesis relevance

The original notes identify four definition-checkable issues: the half-space projection should use a minus sign; polyhedral boundary is a union of feasible faces, not an intersection of supporting hyperplanes; second-order-cone examples omit some branches; and a similarity-transform proof repeats the wrong function subscript. For a unit-normal half-space,

QH(x)={x−a(⟨a,x⟩−b)}.Q_H(x)=\{x-a(\langle a,x\rangle-b)\}.

For the thesis, use this paper to define exact distance, normal validity, nonsmooth branches, and the limits of Boolean distance formulas. Its convex-set results do not automatically extend to nonconvex deformed assemblies, sampled fields, or finite-precision certificates. Preserve these distinctions when designing query types and validation cases.
