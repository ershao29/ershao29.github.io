Research Topics in Implicit Geometry and Neutron Transport

My assessment: implicit modeling is most valuable when complex material partitions, reliable geometry queries, and geometry error control are combined in a representation shared by Monte Carlo and deterministic solvers. A different surface notation alone is insufficient for a strong doctoral topic.

Two facts directly affect topic positioning:

  • Conventional Monte Carlo CSG already constitutes implicit modeling. OpenMC represents surfaces by f(x,y,z)=0f(x,y,z)=0 and combines half-spaces through intersection, union, and complement; MCNP follows similar ideas. Specify whether the proposed object is general implicit surfaces, distance fields, multi-material level sets, or neural representations. OpenMC geometry methods
  • Deterministic methods do not inherently require fitted meshes. SNS_N specifies angular discretization; spatial discretization has several alternatives. Immersed-body methods and adaptive meshes have already been applied to neutron transport without fitting every internal structure. Buchan et al., 2019

The following develops research directions from concrete problems and distinguishes prior work from opportunities.

Three issues deserve closer attention.

First, testing containment in a known solid differs from locating a material region across an entire reactor.

Evaluating the sign of f(𝐱)f(\mathbf x) for one known object may be cheap. Large models contain many solids, hierarchies, and repeated instances, raising questions:

  • Which candidate solids should be queried?
  • How are simultaneous inside classifications handled?
  • At shared interfaces, how does direction determine the next state?
  • Do same-material instances have different temperatures, depletion zones, or tallies?

Implicit representations still need spatial indexes, hierarchies, and region semantics. Combining every component into one function does not automatically yield efficient global location.

Second, shortest spatial boundary distance differs from directional boundary distance.

Let d(𝐱)d(\mathbf x) be signed distance and 𝛀\boldsymbol\Omega a unit flight direction. Surface tracking needs the next valid ray event:

𝐫(s)=𝐱+s𝛀.\mathbf r(s)=\mathbf x+s\boldsymbol\Omega .

Solving f(𝐫(s))=0f(\mathbf r(s))=0 must also determine:

  • Is this the nearest valid intersection or a later one?
  • Is the ray tangent or truly crossing?
  • Is this an auxiliary Boolean surface or an actual region boundary?
  • Is there a thin material layer between adjacent crossings?

Identical endpoint materials can hide an intervening thin layer. Endpoint labels or sign-change checks alone can miss it.

Third, measure geometry errors through physical responses.

For a fixed ray and piecewise-constant cross sections, misclassifying length δℓ\delta\ell of B as A gives optical-thickness error

δτ=(Σt,A−Σt,B)δℓ.\delta\tau = \left(\Sigma_{t,A}-\Sigma_{t,B}\right)\delta\ell .

Since uncollided transmission is T=e−τT=e^{-\tau}, small perturbations give

δTT≈−δτ.\frac{\delta T}{T}\approx-\delta\tau .

The same displacement can have very different effects in weak absorbers and strongly absorbing thin layers. Gap connectivity changes can exceed what a small surface-deviation metric describes.

Evaluate local rates, power, leakage, and streaming as well as shape similarity, total volume, and keffk_{\mathrm{eff}}. Geometry-approximation bias does not vanish with more Monte Carlo particles.

Distinguish general implicit functions, SDFs, and approximations.

For a sphere:

f(𝐱)=∥𝐱−𝐜∥2−R2,d(𝐱)=∥𝐱−𝐜∥−R.f(\mathbf x)=\|\mathbf x-\mathbf c\|^2-R^2, \qquad d(\mathbf x)=\|\mathbf x-\mathbf c\|-R .

Both functions describe the same zero set; only the latter is its SDF. General implicit values cannot be used directly as spatial steps.

Additional qualifications:

  • Boolean combinations generally cease to be exact SDFs; safe stepping depends on lower bounds or Lipschitz properties.
  • Interpolated/neural approximate SDFs may overestimate distance and cross interfaces.
  • An exterior-shape SDF does not contain every internal material interface.
  • Smooth Booleans can move surfaces, round corners, or change thickness.
  • Implicit geometry reduces explicit-surface maintenance but still requires volume, interface-integration, and topology algorithms.

These problems motivate six research directions.

  1. Hierarchical hybrid implicit representations for multi-material transport.

    Addresses expression complexity, material partitions, and multiscale repetition.

    Retain analytic CSG/lattice reuse for regular rods/assemblies; use local level sets, spline implicit fields, or adaptive distance fields for free-form/bent parts. Preserve shared interfaces, adjacent regions, instance IDs, and physical properties.

    Research thin-layer/contact preservation at specified error, overlap/void detection, and local updates after local changes.

    Hybrid geometry has precedents, including Shift's hierarchical CAD/CSG. Focus novelty on implicit-representation error constraints, material consistency, and query performance. Biondo et al., 2023

  2. Point location and first-intersection queries with verifiable errors.

    Recommended first priority, directly useful for MC and MOC.

    Combine conservative exclusion with local accurate solutions: skip certified interface-free space, then use root isolation, intervals, or analytics near interfaces, including thin layers, grazing rays, and coincidence.

    If ff has a reliable Lipschitz upper bound LL over the relevant region, its zero-set distance satisfies

    dist(𝐱,{f=0})≥|f(𝐱)|L.\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf x,\{f=0\}) \ge \frac{|f(\mathbf x)|}{L}.

    For computed f̃\widetilde f with a validated bound |f̃−f|≤εf|\widetilde f-f|\le\varepsilon_f, construct the conservative step

    ssafe=max(0,|f̃(𝐱)|−εf)L.s_{\mathrm{safe}} = \frac{\max(0,|\widetilde f(\mathbf x)|-\varepsilon_f)}{L}.

    Guarantees require valid bounds, domains, and coverage of every relevant interface. The challenge is obtaining tight, inexpensive error bounds.

    Conservative sphere tracing is established, and DAGMC-SDF prefiltering dates to 2017. Transport sphere tracing alone is insufficient novelty; address materials, degeneracy, proofs, and performance failure. Hart, Shriwise et al., 2017

  3. Response-based geometry error estimation and adaptation.

    Uniform tolerances ignore spatial importance.

    Which interfaces need refinement to meet local-power or leakage targets?

    Combine displacement error, cross-section contrast, and forward/adjoint information in an indicator. For small differentiable, topology-preserving perturbations, begin with shape sensitivity:

    δR≈∫ΓGR(𝐱)δn(𝐱)dS.\delta R \approx \int_\Gamma G_R(\mathbf x)\, \delta n(\mathbf x)\,\mathrm dS .

    Here GRG_R measures response sensitivity to normal interface displacement; refine fields or root tolerances by response contribution.

    Begin with computable estimators, then investigate conservative bounds. Channel opening/closing requires separate topology treatment. Existing neutron geometry-sensitivity methods provide a basis. Variansyah et al., 2025 preprint

  4. Implicit interfaces coupled to unfitted deterministic discretization.

    Geometry-fitting can create many cells where the physical solution needs less resolution.

    Carry the solution on background cells KK, define materials by Ωm\Omega_m, and study cut-domain integration/discretization:

    ∫K∩Ωm(⋯)dV,∫K∩Γ(⋯)dS.\int_{K\cap\Omega_m}(\cdots)\,\mathrm dV, \qquad \int_{K\cap\Gamma}(\cdots)\,\mathrm dS .

    Questions include conservative exchange, high-order integration, tiny-cut-cell stability, positivity, and the diffusion limit.

    SNS_N also requires sweep analysis: curved/complex cells can create cyclic upwind dependencies, so improved geometry does not automatically speed the solver. High-order curved-mesh sweeps

    High-order implicit integration and cut-cell stabilization exist; efficient conservative multi-material neutron transport still needs focused research. Saye, 2015, Engwer et al.

    Initially validate integration/conservation on diffusion interfaces, then SNS_N; an existing MOC platform favors implicit segmentation first.

  5. Multiphysics consistency and shape sensitivities under changing geometry.

    Addresses repeated reconstruction for thermal deformation, displacement, and parameter scans.

    Implicit fields enable continuous parameterization/local changes, but require volume accuracy, density/nuclide conservation, conservative field transfer, and incremental track/acceleration updates.

    Differentiable geometry functions do not automatically yield correct transport derivatives. Material jumps, collision sampling, and boundary crossings require shape derivatives, adjoints, or derivative-source methods.

    Start with sequences of static parameterized geometries before moving interfaces in time.

  6. Neural implicit geometry with reliability constraints.

    Addresses complex free-form compression, scan reconstruction, and similar instances.

    DeepSDF demonstrates continuous neural shape representations, but its objectives differ from reliable transport geometry. DeepSDF, 2019

    Research thin-layer/topology protection, local errors, and neural proposals verified by reliable algorithms.

    Treat this as a higher-risk extension. Neural inference may be slower than simple analytics; include training, preprocessing, and validation costs.

For a doctoral focus, prioritize reliable queries and response error control.

A possible title:

Implicit Geometry Queries and Error Control for Complex Multi-Material Neutron Transport.

Three successive questions: preserve material interfaces; locate/intersect efficiently and reliably; allocate geometry accuracy by physical response. This combines computational geometry with reactor-physics criteria.

Separate two comparisons:

  • Fixed geometry, changed query algorithm: speed, memory, query reliability.
  • Changed representation accuracy: physical-response effects with controlled statistical/discretization errors.

The earliest testable hypothesis is whether adaptive implicit geometry lowers model preparation + queries + transport cost while preserving layers/topology and meeting a response-error target. This identifies both useful implicit regimes and cases favoring analytic CSG or other representations.
